PLNH

BSheoldred, the Apocalypse
Colors
Mono-black
Themes
Making everyone drawPunishing drawsLife drain
Bracket
2 (Core)
Difficulty
Medium

What it wants to do

No deck at the table is more generous, and every gift has a price. Everyone gets extra cards, your opponents pay for every one, and your own draws keep you healthy. Point a big Damnable Pact at one player and they're out of the game.

The commander

Sheoldred, the Apocalypse is a 4/5 with deathtouch that nobody wants to fight, but its real damage is quieter. Whenever you draw a card, you gain 2 life, and whenever an opponent draws one, they lose 2. Sheoldred is the engine, so it draws removal like a magnet. Swiftfoot Boots gives it hexproof, and Malakir Rebirth can save it from a kill spell.

Opening hand

  • Sheoldred costs four. Three lands and a two-mana rock like Arcane Signet get it out on turn three.
  • Card draw that's just for you, like Phyrexian Arena, is great early. Save the cards that make everyone draw until Sheoldred is out, or you're feeding three opponents for free.
  • Mulligan hands that can't reach four mana by turn four.

Key cards

  • Underworld Dreams, Fate Unraveler, Scrawling Crawler and Ob Nixilis, the Hate-Twisted are smaller Sheoldreds: each card an opponent draws costs them 1 more. They keep the pressure on when Sheoldred is gone.
  • Howling Mine, Font of Mythos and Seizan, Perverter of Truth give everyone extra cards every turn, and Sheoldred bills each one.
  • Spells that say "target player draws", like Damnable Pact, can refill your hand, but they're best aimed at an opponent. After the first cast, Decorum Dissertation gives you a free copy on each of your turns.
  • Bloodletter of Aclazotz doubles all the life your opponents lose on your turn.
  • Vito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose and Sanguine Bond turn the life you gain into life an opponent loses.

How it wins

Most games, the table bleeds out a little at a time until Exsanguinate, Torment of Hailfire or Gray Merchant of Asphodel can finish it. Cabal Coffers and Crypt Ghast make the mana for a big X.

The quick kill is Damnable Pact aimed at one opponent. They draw X cards and lose X life, and Sheoldred takes 2 more for each card: 3X in all. With Bloodletter out it's 6X, so seven mana takes 30 life.

What beats it

  • Killing Sheoldred again and again. It comes back from the command zone, but costs 2 more each time, and the smaller Sheoldreds only do half its job.
  • Opponents who put your gifts to work, and win with them before the life loss catches up.
  • Artifact and enchantment removal: most of the engines are one or the other.
  • Your own life total. Cards like Greed charge you life, and without Sheoldred's lifegain, that adds up.
  • Cards that stop players drawing more than one card a turn shut off most of the plan.

Sideboard

For a Bracket 3 table, take out Price of Knowledge and put in Peer into the Abyss. It makes a player draw half their library and lose half their life. Aimed at an opponent while Sheoldred is out, that's a kill: they draw a pile of cards at 2 life each. Even without Sheoldred, Bloodletter of Aclazotz makes it a kill, and Underworld Dreams or Ob Nixilis usually do. That's a two-card kill with your commander, which is why it waits for Bracket 3.

The Most Dangerous Gaymer

BGThe Most Dangerous Gamer
Colors
Golgari: black and green
Themes
+1/+1 countersOpening AttractionsCommander damage
Bracket
2 (Core)
Difficulty
Medium

What it wants to do

Aptronym (noun): look it up, nerd. Attractions are artifacts kept in a deck of their own. Opening one puts the top card onto the battlefield. On your turn, you roll a die, and every Attraction with that number lit up gives you a little something, like a card or a token. Each one opened while TMDG is out gives it a +1/+1 counter, and the deck multiplies them until it can knock out a player with 21 commander damage.

The commander

The Most Dangerous Gamer is a 2/2 for four that fights dirty: with deathtouch, any creature it damages dies. It opens an Attraction when it enters or attacks, and gets a +1/+1 counter whenever you open one, its own or not. Claiming an Attraction's prize lets it destroy a permanent, but only Pick-a-Beeble has a prize here, so that's a bonus.

Opening hand

  • TMDG costs four, with black and green. Sol Ring, Llanowar Elves or Rampant Growth speeds it up.
  • An early Hardened Scales or Winding Constrictor adds one extra to every batch of counters that follows.
  • One land is never enough, however good the rest looks.

Key cards

  • Corpsejack Menace and Doubling Season double every batch of counters. With Doubling Season and Hardened Scales, each Attraction gives TMDG four.
  • At each of your combats, Ouroboroid puts counters equal to its power on each of your creatures, itself included, so the number climbs every turn.
  • Duskshell Crawler, Kodama of the West Tree and Power Fist give TMDG trample. With deathtouch, one damage kills each blocker, and the rest hits the player.
  • My Precious makes it unblockable, and Canopy Cover nearly so. With either, opponents can't target it.
  • Every Attraction has 6 lit up, so a 6 visits them all. Night Shift of the Living Dead turns a 5 into a 6 for 1 life.

How it wins

One player at a time: 21 combat damage from TMDG knocks a player out. It gains a few counters a turn, so the first knockout takes several swings, and each one after comes faster.

When counters pile up, the whole team joins in. Bristly Bill, Spine Sower doubles the counters on each of your creatures for five mana, and Swinging Ship, an Attraction, adds a second combat.

What beats it

  • A kill spell on TMDG before it grows. Swiftfoot Boots and Snakeskin Veil give it hexproof, and if it dies, Host of the Hereafter or The Ozolith passes its counters to another creature.
  • Board wipes take every counter with them. Save Heroic Intervention or Inspiring Call for them.
  • Combo decks and fast armies. The deck has three removal cards, like Black Hole and Saw in Half, and one nine-mana wipe, so it can't slow them down much.
  • Chump blockers, until TMDG has trample.

Blink Bear

WUBRGHei Bai, Forest Guardian
Colors
All five colors
Themes
Blinking your commanderStacking ShrinesSpirit tokens
Bracket
3 (Upgraded)
Difficulty
Hard

What it wants to do

Now you see it, now you don't. This is a blink deck and a Shrine deck in one. Blink spells exile Hei Bai and bring it straight back, and each time it returns, it brings a Shrine with it. Shrines get stronger the more of them you control: one barely matters, but a pile of them takes over the game.

The commander

Hei Bai, Forest Guardian is a 4/4 for four who never arrives alone. When it enters, it digs through your library to the first Shrine and puts that Shrine onto the battlefield. For one mana of each color and a tap, it makes a 1/1 Spirit for each legendary enchantment you control, and every Shrine in the deck is one. Only Spirits can block them. Blink it when a removal spell targets it: the spell misses, and you get another Shrine.

Opening hand

  • Birds of Paradise, Llanowar Elves and Delighted Halfling get Hei Bai out a turn early, on turn three.
  • Each fetch land can grab a two- or three-color land, so five colors is easier than it looks.
  • A one-mana blink spell like Cloudshift or Ephemerate gets a second Shrine soon after it lands.
  • Throw back hands with no green source: Hei Bai and the early mana creatures all need it.

Key cards

  • Flicker and Flicker of Fate can blink a Shrine instead: Northern Air Temple drains the table again, and The Spirit Oasis draws you another handful.
  • Soulherder blinks a creature at the end of each of your turns. Once you pay six to harness The Mind Stone, it does the same, and can pick a Shrine.
  • Sanctum of All puts a Shrine onto the battlefield every upkeep, and once you control six, the other Shrines' abilities trigger twice.
  • Starfield Vocalist doubles your abilities that trigger when something enters, so Hei Bai finds two Shrines at a time.

How it wins

Slowly, then all at once. On each of your turns, Honden of Infinite Rage deals damage equal to the number of Shrines you control, Sanctum of Stone Fangs drains each opponent for the same, and Honden of Life's Web makes that many Spirits. Impact Tremors and Purphoros, God of the Forge hit every opponent whenever one of your creatures enters, tokens and blinks included. Hei Bai's Spirits finish the job.

What beats it

  • Mass enchantment removal: one spell takes every Shrine, and the cards that tax attackers too.
  • Anything that stops abilities from triggering when a creature enters turns Hei Bai into a plain 4/4.
  • Speed. Stacking Shrines takes a few turns, and until then Ghostly Prison, Propaganda and Sphere of Safety make attacking you expensive.
  • Creature wipes take Hei Bai and the Spirits, but most Shrines stay. Teferi's Protection saves the whole board, and Eerie Interlude brings Hei Bai back with another Shrine.

Get Sucked

BVito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose
Colors
Mono-black
Themes
Gaining lifeDraining opponentsVampires
Bracket
3 (Upgraded)
Difficulty
Medium

What it wants to do

It's death by a thousand cuts. The deck slowly drains life from the table, and Vito (and some of its friends) turns lifegain into damage. With the right combo in place, one small gain becomes a loop that ends the game. Or drop a bomb and kill a player in a single turn.

The commander

Vito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose is low-cost, but fragile. Each time you gain life, it makes an opponent of your choice lose that much, so you can spread the damage or focus one player. For five mana it gives your creatures lifelink for the turn, feeding its ping effect. Protect it with Swiftfoot Boots.

Opening hand

  • Keep three or four lands, or two lands and a cheap mana rock.
  • Look for a cheap way to gain life, such as Vampire of the Dire Moon, Creeping Bloodsucker or Pristine Talisman.
  • Send back hands with one land or only expensive cards. Vito starts in the command zone, so you never need it in hand.

Key cards

  • Exquisite Blood and Bloodthirsty Conqueror: whenever an opponent loses life, you gain that much. With Vito, that's the loop.
  • Sanguine Bond and Enduring Tenacity do Vito's job if it's removed.
  • Gray Merchant of Asphodel drains each opponent for every black mana symbol on your permanents, and Vito sends the total you gain at one player.
  • Whip of Erebos gives all your creatures lifelink.
  • Bloodletter of Aclazotz doubles what opponents lose on your turn.

How it wins

Usually slowly: your drains hit everyone each turn, Vito adds a second hit to each, and Exsanguinate or Torment of Hailfire finishes whoever is left.

The fast way: with Vito and Exquisite Blood or Bloodthirsty Conqueror out, any lifegain starts a loop. An opponent loses 1, you gain 1, and so on until every opponent is at 0. It runs until the game ends unless someone removes a piece. Both combo pieces cost five, so it rarely happens before turn five or six.

Blood Tribute kills one player: tap Vito to kick it, and they lose half their life, you gain it, and Vito takes care of the rest.

What beats it

  • Anything that stops you gaining life shuts the deck down.
  • Instant-speed removal can break the loop while it runs. Imp's Mischief can redirect one removal spell.
  • Enchantment removal hits Exquisite Blood, Sanguine Bond and Whip of Erebos.
  • With only two board wipes, a wide army can get ahead of you. No Mercy makes attacking you costly.
  • Once the table knows about the loop, expect Vito to die on sight.

Sideboard

For a Bracket 2 table, take out Exquisite Blood, Bloodthirsty Conqueror and Blood Tribute, and put in Pontiff of Blight, Vein Ripper and Grave Venerations. That removes the loop and the OTK.

  • Pontiff gives all your creatures extort, so each spell you cast lets you pay extra black mana to drain everyone a little more.
  • Vein Ripper drains an opponent for 2 whenever any creature dies.
  • Grave Venerations makes you the monarch. While you keep it, you draw an extra card and get a creature card back from your graveyard at the end of each of your turns. It also drains each opponent for 1 whenever one of your creatures dies.

Hit the Pen Too Hard

BRGThe Reaper, King No More
Colors
Jund: black, red and green
Themes
Spreading -1/-1 countersStealing creaturesChain reactions
Bracket
3 (Upgraded)
Difficulty
Hard

What it wants to do

Sometimes dead is better. The deck spreads -1/-1 counters around the table, shrinking creatures until they die, and once a turn the Reaper claims one of the dead for you. Counters can also make tokens or drain life, and with the right three cards out, one counter starts a chain reaction that drains the whole table.

The commander

The Reaper, King No More is a 3/3 Scarecrow with sticky fingers. Its cost looks like six, but with black, red and green it costs only three. When it enters, it puts a -1/-1 counter on up to two creatures. Once each turn, when an opponent's creature with a -1/-1 counter dies, you may put it onto the battlefield on your side. You keep it even if the Reaper dies.

Opening hand

  • Three lands and a two-drop is a fine start. Delighted Halfling makes any color for the Reaper, so it can arrive on turn two.
  • Missing a color? Pay two mana of any kind in its place.
  • Dread Tiller and Spitting Dilophosaurus put counters on enemy creatures early, giving the Reaper something to steal.

Key cards

  • Necroskitter steals like the Reaper, with no once-a-turn limit. With it out, Black Sun's Zenith wipes the board and hands you every creature your opponents lose.
  • Village Pillagers has wither, so when it enters, every opposing creature gets a -1/-1 counter.
  • Maha, Its Feathers Night sets every opposing creature's base toughness to 1, so a single counter kills most of them.
  • Nest of Scarabs, Hapatra, Vizier of Poisons and Flourishing Defenses turn your counters into 1/1 tokens. Obelisk Spider drains each opponent for 1 whenever you place counters.

How it wins

The everyday win is a slow-motion heist: the Reaper takes a creature a turn, tokens pile up, and Obelisk Spider and Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER drain the table. Spitting Dilophosaurus keeps enemy creatures with counters from blocking.

The fast kill is a chain reaction. Get Blowfly Infestation out alongside one of the token makers above, then put a counter on a 1/1 token. It dies, so Blowfly puts a counter on another 1/1, and that counter makes a fresh token to go next. Add Sephiroth, Obelisk Spider or All Will Be One, and the laps drain everyone else to zero. To stop, put the counter on something bigger. It usually comes late, and tutors like Demonic Tutor help find the pieces.

What beats it

  • Graveyard hate that exiles dying creatures: nothing dies, so nothing comes back.
  • Blowfly Infestation, Nest of Scarabs and Flourishing Defenses fall to anything that hits enchantments.
  • Decks with few creatures leave the Reaper nothing to take, and +1/+1 counters cancel -1/-1 counters one for one.
  • Repeated removal on the Reaper. Deflecting Swat costs nothing while it's out, and Heroic Intervention protects your whole board for a turn.

Tiny Boner

BTinybones, the Pickpocket
Colors
Mono-black
Themes
Making everyone discardPunishing discardsStealing from graveyards
Bracket
3 (Upgraded)
Difficulty
Hard

What it wants to do

Nobody at the table gets to keep their cards, not even you. Everyone discards, turn after turn, and every card an opponent throws away costs them life. Tinybones picks through their graveyards and plays the best finds itself. Once their hands are empty, the other Tinybones takes 10 life from each of them.

The commander

Tinybones, the Pickpocket is cheap, deadly and very small: one mana for a 1/1 with deathtouch. Whenever it deals combat damage to a player, you may cast a creature, artifact, enchantment or planeswalker from that player's graveyard, paying with any kind of mana. You cast it right then, so keep mana open when Tinybones attacks. A blocker stops it cold, so Brotherhood Regalia and Whispersilk Cloak, which make it unblockable, are its best friends.

Opening hand

  • Keep three lands, or two lands and a cheap rock like Sol Ring or Arcane Signet.
  • Cheap discard like Thoughtseize, Hymn to Tourach or Burglar Rat starts filling graveyards for Tinybones to raid.
  • An unblockable equipment is worth keeping a hand for. Tinybones costs one, so it can start attacking on turn two.

Key cards

  • Bottomless Pit, Necrogen Mists, Cunning Lethemancer and Oppression make every player discard again and again, you included. Play out your own hand first, then put them down.
  • Megrim, Liliana's Caress, Raiders' Wake and Fell Specter each take 2 life from an opponent for every card they discard. With one out, Dark Deal (everyone discards their hand and draws one fewer) costs each opponent 2 life per card they held.
  • Waste Not and Geth's Grimoire turn their discards into cards, mana and Zombies for you. Bone Miser does the same with yours.
  • Fireshrieker gives Tinybones double strike: two hits, two thefts.

How it wins

Most games, the table bleeds 2 life at a time while Tinybones helps itself to their discards. When hands run dry, Tinybones, Trinket Thief closes it out: for six mana, each opponent with no cards in hand loses 10, as often as you can pay. Cabal Coffers and Crypt Ghast make that mana, or pay for a big Exsanguinate or Torment of Hailfire. Torment is cruel here: a player with no cards can't discard to it, so each time they lose 3 life or sacrifice something that isn't a land.

What beats it

  • Enchantment removal: most of the discard engines and punishers are enchantments.
  • Graveyard removal leaves Tinybones nothing to steal.
  • Decks that want cards in the graveyard, like reanimator, thank you for the discard.
  • Fast decks full of cheap cards empty their own hands before the discard matters.
  • Your own life total: Thoughtseize, Infernal Grasp and Black Market Connections all charge life.
  • Tinybones is a 1/1, so everything kills it. Swiftfoot Boots and Lightning Greaves protect it, and it's cheap to recast.

Lamaplication

WURElsha, Threefold Master
Colors
Jeskai: white, blue and red
Themes
Casting cheap spellsMultiplying MonksCombat buffs
Bracket
4 (Optimized)
Difficulty
Hard

What it wants to do

One monk walks in. A whole monastery walks out. A quarter of the deck costs one mana, and each noncreature spell you cast makes Elsha bigger for the turn. Every time Elsha hits a player, it recruits a Monk for each point of damage, and your spells pump them too. With double strike and a few spells, Elsha can knock out a player in one combat.

The commander

Elsha, Threefold Master is a scrawny 1/1 for three that bulks up fast. It has trample and prowess: each noncreature spell you cast gives it +1/+1 until end of turn. Whenever it deals combat damage to a player, you create that many 1/1 Monks, and they have prowess too. It's the whole engine, and everyone knows it.

Opening hand

  • Elsha needs blue, red and white on turn three. Every fetch land here can find any of the three, and Arcane Signet makes any of them. White is the one you'll most often lack.
  • Look for two or three one-mana spells that draw a card and help Elsha connect, like Slip Through Space (unblockable) or Expedite (haste).

Key cards

  • Monastery Mentor makes a Monk for every noncreature spell, no combat needed.
  • Bria, Riptide Rogue gives your other creatures prowess, so each Monk gets two, and every noncreature spell makes one of your creatures unblockable.
  • Jeskai Ascendancy gives your whole team +1/+1 for every noncreature spell.
  • Roaming Throne, naming Monk, makes Elsha's token ability and every Monk's prowess trigger twice. Veyran, Voice of Duality doubles whatever your instants and sorceries set off.
  • Boros Charm and Psychotic Fury give Elsha double strike: two hits, two batches of Monks.

How it wins

Through the front door, mostly. Elsha connects, the Monks pile up, and every spell pumps the army.

The quick kill is commander damage: 21 combat damage from one commander knocks a player out. With double strike, Elsha needs just 11 power, and Jeskai Ascendancy or Roaming Throne makes each spell add 2.

Purphoros, God of the Forge, Impact Tremors and Warleader's Call hit every opponent whenever a creature enters on your side. A 6/6 double-striking Elsha makes twelve Monks: 24 damage to each opponent from Purphoros alone.

For one huge turn, Underworld Breach, Mizzix's Mastery and Will of the Jeskai replay your graveyard's spells, and Vivi Ornitier pings every opponent for each.

What beats it

  • Removal on Elsha in response to your pump spells wastes the whole turn. Fierce Guardianship and Force of Will can counter it without spending mana.
  • Board wipes sweep away the Monks. Teferi's Protection and Clever Concealment hide your board until your next turn, and Boros Charm can make it indestructible.
  • Graveyard removal shuts off the big turn.
  • Taxes on spells, or limits on how many you can cast, choke the deck.

Rob Mills

UNeerdiv, Devious Diver
Colors
Mono-blue
Themes
MillingUntapping your commanderCasting from the graveyard
Bracket
4 (Optimized)
Difficulty
Hard

What it wants to do

Ever wanted an anime mermaid to get your deck wet? Wish granted! Milling moves cards from the top of a library to the graveyard, and anyone who has to draw from an empty library loses. Neerdiv mills a little with every tap, until a loop taps and untaps it endlessly, emptying every opponent's library in one turn. Until then, the deck counters threats and digs for the pieces.

The commander

Neerdiv, Devious Diver starts as a modest 2/2, but it never stops growing. Whenever it becomes tapped, a player you choose mills cards equal to its power. Whenever you cast a spell from your graveyard, or use an ability of a card there, you draw a card and Neerdiv gets a +1/+1 counter. Every flashback spell makes the next mill bigger.

Opening hand

  • Two or three lands and cheap spells will do: Neerdiv costs three, and most spells cost less.
  • Mystic Remora and Rhystic Study draw you a card for opponents' spells unless they pay extra. Both are best early.
  • A tutor or a loop piece is a bonus.
  • Send back hands with one land or no card draw.

Key cards

  • Freed from the Real and Pemmin's Aura untap Neerdiv for one blue mana. Freed can also tap it, so after High Tide, each Island you tap is another mill.
  • Paradise Mantle and Relic of Legends let Neerdiv tap for mana. Either one plus either aura is the loop.
  • Agatha's Soul Cauldron gives Neerdiv the abilities of creature cards it exiles. Exile Aphetto Alchemist, and Neerdiv can tap to untap itself, over and over: a second loop.
  • Quiet Speculation puts three flashback spells from your library into your graveyard. Snapcaster Mage and Mission Briefing let you cast others from there.

How it wins

With the loop together, tap Neerdiv for blue mana, and a player you choose mills. Spend that mana to untap it, and repeat as often as you like. Empty every opponent's library: they lose when they next draw, or right away if you make them. Archmage's Charm and Deep Analysis each make a player draw two.

With Thassa's Oracle in hand, mill yourself instead, then cast it with your library empty: you win on the spot.

Without the loop, Neerdiv mills with each attack, and High Tide plus Freed takes a big bite out of a library.

What beats it

  • Removal on Neerdiv, worst of all while it wears an aura: the aura goes too. Swiftfoot Boots and Pemmin's Aura stop removal that targets it, and March of Swirling Mist can hide it until your next turn.
  • Artifact and enchantment removal: the loop needs an aura and an artifact.
  • Graveyard decks. Milling them hands them cards to bring back, so save Tormod's Crypt for them.
  • Wide boards: the deck has few answers to creatures, and only Cyclonic Rift clears the table.
